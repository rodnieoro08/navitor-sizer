How a size is chosen

  1. Core decision is perimeter and perimeter-derived diameter. Perimeter-derived diameter = perimeter / π, rounded to 1 decimal. Area and entered mean diameter are checks only.
  2. Hard filters. A size is excluded if SOV min width is below the floor (25/27/29/31/34), ascending aorta is outside that size’s range, or coronary height is below 10 mm.
  3. Range match. A size is a candidate only when the perimeter sits in its perimeter range. Score is 0.70 perimeter + 0.30 perimeter-derived diameter against that size’s annulus band.
  4. Soft penalties (score and flags): ellipticity < 0.73; SOV width < 2 mm above floor; STJ < labelled diameter; moderate/severe STJ calcium; severe LVOT calcium; access below FlexNav minimum. Minimum coronary height is 10 mm — below that excludes the size.
  5. Consistency check. Perimeter-derived Ø = peri / π. Area-derived Ø = 2√(area/π). If these disagree with each other or with entered mean Ø by > 2 mm, the result is flagged — fix the tracing before arguing about 25 vs 27.
  6. Access. 23 and 25 mm need ≥ 5.0 mm vessel. 27, 29 and 35 mm need ≥ 5.5 mm. Below that is a warning, not always an exclude (alternative access exists).
  7. SOV height is not used. It is not asked for, not scored and not flagged. SOV diameter is the root measurement used.

Field logic at shared edges

Rodnie’s field logic, not an Abbott claim. The Heart Team decides.

  1. The smaller valve is the default. Oversizing is shown for both (π × label ÷ perimeter − 1). The tie-breakers below are checked in this order and each is shown on the Result as favours smaller / favours larger / neutral / veto larger / not entered.
  2. Calcium burden & distribution (first). Cusp or annular calcium moderate/severe, eccentric leaflet calcium, or moderate/severe LVOT calcium supports the larger valve. Protruding LVOT calcium vetoes the larger (rupture and conduction risk).
  3. Annular area as a check on perimeter. Below the larger size’s area range = eccentric annulus, perimeter flatters the larger → veto larger. Inside both ranges → neutral. Above the smaller size’s range → the smaller has been left → favours larger.
  4. SOV diameter. Mean of L/R/NC, then the smallest sinus. Below the larger’s floor → veto (hard exclude). Mean or smallest < larger floor + 2 mm → tight for the larger. Genuinely uniform = all three entered and max − min ≤ 2 mm. “SOV min width only” cannot show uniformity. A single narrow sinus keeps the smaller valve.
  5. LVOT vs annulus. LVOT smaller than the perimeter-derived diameter constrains the larger valve’s inflow → favours smaller and blocks a step-up.
  6. Coronary height & STJ. Veto a size more often than they choose one (hard excludes above). STJ smaller than the larger label favours the smaller.
  7. Conduction risk vs PPM. RBBB / short membranous septum / heavy septal calcium favours the smaller valve and a higher implant, and blocks a step-up. Small patient / low expected EOA favours the larger only if the root can take it (no veto, SOV not tight).
  8. Decision. Any veto → smaller. SOV tight (or not entered) → smaller unless all of: calcium more than mild, area ≥ larger’s area minimum, LVOT entered and not smaller than the annulus, three uniform sinuses, no conduction risk. SOV roomy → step up if calcium supports it, area is above the smaller’s range, or PPM concern — and LVOT is not smaller and there is no conduction risk.
  9. Example: 66 mm, SOV 27. 23 (60–66) and 25 (66–73). Perimeter-derived Ø 21.0 mm; circular annulus ≈ 346.6 mm²; oversizing ≈ 9.5% for 23, ≈ 19% for 25. A 27 mm sinus is tight for a 25. Default 23; 25 only if calcium is more than mild, area ≥ 338, LVOT ≥ 21.0 and all three sinuses are about 27.

Classic at shared edges

  1. Overlap perimeters. 66 · 72–73 · 79 · 85 mm belong to two sizes. Both stay candidates. The higher score wins only if the gap is ≥ 0.08; otherwise the app returns a co-primary pair for the Heart Team.
  2. 66 mm peri: 23 and 25 both legal on peri. Prefer 25 if area ≥ 338 and mean Ø ≥ 21 and SOV ≥ 27 and coronaries comfortable. Prefer 23 if area stays in 277–346, SOV is tight for 25, LCA/RCA low, or STJ calcium is significant.
  3. 72–73 mm peri: 25 and 27. 27 needs SOV ≥ 29 and access ≥ 5.5. If area is still ≤ 415 and mean Ø ≤ 23, 25 is usually cleaner.
  4. 79 mm peri: 27 and 29. 29 needs SOV ≥ 31 and AA 32–42. Do not upsize if coronaries are low or STJ is small.
  5. 85 mm peri: 29 and 35. 35 needs SOV ≥ 34 and mean Ø 27–30. 35 AA window is wide (27–44).

What this will not do

It will not read a blurry theatre photo of a 3mensio monitor and implant a valve. It will not replace virtual valve / VTC in 3mensio for coronary obstruction. It will not size bicuspids, ViV, or non-Navitor platforms. It will not store identifiable patient data on a server. Not official Abbott software. Not CE/UKCA marked. The Heart Team decides.
